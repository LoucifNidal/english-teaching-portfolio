Comparing Animals

A colourful, communicative lesson helping young learners use comparative adjectives to describe and compare animals confidently.

Lesson Details

Lesson Type Grammar / Speaking
Level A1–A2 Elementary
Learners Young Learners (9–11 years old)
Number of Learners 6–12, adaptable for larger classes
Lesson Length 45 minutes
Main Skill Speaking
Supporting Skills Listening, Grammar, Vocabulary
Topic Comparing Animals
Teaching Approach Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) using a Presentation–Practice–Production (PPP) framework
Language Focus Comparative adjectives: bigger than, smaller than, faster than, slower than, taller than, shorter than, heavier than, lighter than

Aim & Learning Objectives

To help learners understand and use comparative adjectives confidently when describing and comparing animals.

By the end of the lesson learners will be able to:

  • Recognize common comparative adjectives.
  • Compare two animals using complete sentences.
  • Ask and answer simple comparison questions.
  • Participate in a speaking game using comparative language.

Success Criteria

An elephant is bigger than a dog.
A rabbit is faster than a turtle.
A giraffe is taller than a horse.

Learners should communicate confidently rather than memorising fixed sentences.

Anticipated Problems & Solutions

Problem 1 — Comparative forms

Learners may forget to use -er or more. Model the correct sentence, highlight the comparative adjective and provide examples.

Problem 2 — Confusing comparisons

Use large animal visuals and encourage learners to justify their answers.

Problem 3 — Shy learners

Begin with pair work before moving to small-group activities. Offer praise and encouragement.

Problem 4 — Fast finishers

Challenge faster learners to create their own comparisons and follow-up questions.

Lesson Stages

1. Warm-up & Lead-in · 5 min

Interaction: T ↔ Ss

Use animal flashcards, simple questions and an optional action chant. Introduce the lesson as an “animal detective” challenge.

2. Presentation & Elicitation · 8 min

Interaction: T ↔ Ss

Compare two animals and guide learners toward complete comparative sentences.

3. MFP · 7 min

Interaction: T ↔ Ss

Clarify meaning, form and pronunciation using CCQs, modelling and repetition.

4. Controlled Practice · 7 min

Interaction: Individual → Pairs → T ↔ Ss

Learners complete worksheet activities, compare answers and receive feedback.

5. Animal Challenge Game · 8 min

Interaction: Groups

Teams compare displayed animals and earn points for complete correct sentences.

6. Freer Speaking · 8 min

Interaction: Pairs → Whole Class

Pairs choose animals, create three comparisons and present one to the class.

7. Feedback & Error Correction · 3 min

Interaction: T ↔ Ss

Review anonymous learner examples and guide learners toward corrections.

8. Review & Exit Check · 2 min

Interaction: T ↔ Ss

Display final animals and invite complete comparative sentences.

Timing note: The detailed stages total 48 minutes. Activities can be shortened slightly if a strict 45-minute lesson is required.

Rules

  1. Display two animals.
  2. Ask one comparison question.
  3. Learners discuss briefly.
  4. One learner answers using a complete sentence.
  5. Award one point for a correct sentence.
  6. Continue with different animal pairs.

Optional challenge: Invite learners to create their own comparison questions.
